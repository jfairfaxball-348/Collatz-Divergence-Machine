## 1. Executive result

T23 does **not** restore universal exact relation lifting for genuinely active unequal-growth reducible systems.

It does prove two precise multiscale analytic statements that sharpen the T22 boundary.

Let
\[
q_n=q_{a+Rn}

Write

I_{>i}

### T23.1 — slowest-face elimination

If an algebraic-coefficient toric analytic function \(f\) vanishes on an infinite subsequence of the exact reduced orbit, then its restriction to the slowest growth boundary
\[
f\bmod I_{>1}
\]
vanishes identically on the slow projected torus.

The proof uses Corvaja–Zannier Proposition 3, not their full Theorem 3 on the original multiscale point.

The key observation is that the remainder
\[
f-(f\bmod I_{>1})
\]
is supported on profiles \(>g_1\), hence along the exact orbit it is smaller than every fixed power of the norm of the slow projected point:
\[
\left|
f(q_n)-(f\bmod I_{>1})(q_n)
\right|_2
=
O(\|q_n^{\rm slow}\|_2^B)
\qquad
\text{for every fixed }B>0.
\]
The slow projected coordinates all have one comparable profile \(g_1\), so internally they satisfy the Corvaja–Zannier height/contraction condition. Proposition 3 then forces the slow face to vanish on finitely many torus cosets containing the subsequence. The T18 dense étale orbit has the Bell–Ghioca–Tucker finite-hit property, so every infinite subsequence is Zariski dense; its slow projection is therefore Zariski dense in the projected torus. Hence the slow face vanishes identically.

This is a genuinely multiscale statement: the full point fails the T22 common-scale condition, but the slowest Newton face can still be eliminated.

### T23.2 — principal higher-growth ideal zero theorem

Assume in addition that
\[
I_{>1}=(\theta^\delta)
\]
is principal, generated by one positive monomial of profile \(>g_1\).

Then every nonzero algebraic-coefficient toric analytic function has only finitely many zeros on the exact reduced orbit.

Indeed, an infinite zero subsequence and T23.1 imply
\[
f\in I_{>1},
\qquad
f=\theta^\delta f_1.
\]
Since every orbit point lies in the torus,
\[
\theta^\delta(q_n)\ne0,
\]
so \(f_1\) vanishes on the same infinite subsequence. Repeating T23.1 gives
\[
f\in I_{>1}^j
\]
for every \(j\ge1\). The toric analytic algebra is separated for the positive monomial-adic filtration, hence
\[
\bigcap_{j\ge1}I_{>1}^j=0.
\]
Therefore \(f=0\), contradiction.

Thus a genuine unequal-growth dense-orbit analytic zero theorem is proved for the **principal higher-growth ideal subclass**.

This does **not** yet give the required T17/T18 exact lifting theorem.

T22 established a second independent mismatch in the auxiliary-function proof:
\[
\text{local upper decay}
\sim
\exp(-cP\,g_{\rm slow}(n)),
\]
while the global Liouville lower bound sees
\[
\exp(-CD\,g_{\rm fast}(n)).
\]
T23.2 replaces the zero-set ingredient for its subclass, but it does not automatically replace this quantitative auxiliary-function comparison or produce
\[
Q(\alpha,\mathbf X)=P(\mathbf X).
\]

An \(I_{>1}\)-adic auxiliary construction is now a concrete possible repair, especially when \(I_{>1}\) is principal, but T23 does not prove that construction.

The general nonprincipal case remains open. After the slowest face is removed, the next Newton face has coefficients that are analytic functions of the slower variables. Their values are generally not algebraic numbers, so existing moving-target Subspace Theorems do not apply. Algebraic truncation at the precision needed to see the next scale incurs height on the same order as that next scale, rather than the required \(o(\text{point height})\).

No direct analytic counterexample of Route B was found.

Accordingly T23 closes neither universal unequal-growth lifting nor a new positive-integer anchor class.

### 3.3 Functional matrix

The canonical equation

\mathbf F(x)=\mathcal A(x)\mathbf F(\rho(x))

\overline{\mathbf F}_i(x)
=

Thus the canonical system is **filtered in character support**.

It is not, in general, block triangular in the finite state-function index. Growth class lives in monomial support, not in the state label.

A rational basis change over \(K(Y)\) need not respect the monomial filtration. Denominators can mix growth strata.

Relation ideals inherit an \(I_{>i}\)-adic filtration, but the associated graded pieces are modules over lower-stratum analytic rings. They do not automatically split as independent Mahler systems.

## 4. Associated graded pieces do not automatically decouple

The filtration is preserved by pullback, but max-additivity has an important consequence.

A monomial of profile \(g_i\) multiplied by any monomial of a lower profile still has profile \(g_i\). Hence the \(i\)-th stratum is naturally a module over all lower strata.

Therefore an associated graded piece is not an independent balanced-growth system with constant algebraic coefficients. Its coefficients can be analytic functions on the slower boundary.

This is the exact coupling that blocks naive induction.

Passing to a Rees algebra records the coupling but does not remove it.

## 5. Newton-face analysis

Let
\[
f=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_Y}a_\gamma\theta^\gamma
\]
be a nonzero algebraic-coefficient toric analytic function.

### 5.1 Slowest face

Define
\[
f_{\rm slow}=f\bmod I_{>1}.
\]

All terms outside \(f_{\rm slow}\) have profile at least \(g_2\).

On the exact orbit,
\[
-\log\|q_n^{\rm slow}\|_2=\Theta(g_1(n)),
\]
while every generator of \(I_{>1}\) has
\[
-\log|\theta^\gamma(q_n)|_2
=
\Theta(g_j(n))
\]
for some \(j\ge2\).

Since
\[
g_j(n)/g_1(n)\to\infty,
\]
the high-profile remainder is smaller than every fixed power of the slow norm.

This proves T23.1.

### 5.2 Can the fastest face always be isolated?

The useful face is the **slowest surviving face**, not the fastest face.

At a nonarchimedean attracting boundary, slower-profile monomials are larger and therefore dominate before cancellation. Faster strata are asymptotically invisible until every slower face has been eliminated.

### 5.3 Same-face cancellation

Terms on the same slowest face can cancel infinitely often. T23 does not assume ultrametric uniqueness of one monomial.

Corvaja–Zannier Proposition 3 handles the complete slow analytic face at once.

### 5.4 Induction failure in the nonprincipal case

After \(f_{\rm slow}=0\), one only knows
\[
f\in I_{>1}.
\]

If
\[
I_{>1}=(m_1,\ldots,m_s),
\qquad
s>1,
\]
then
\[
f=\sum_{j=1}^s m_j f_j
\]
does not imply that any \(f_j\) vanishes on the orbit.

This is the precise point where direct induction stops.

### 5.5 Principal ideal exception

If
\[
I_{>1}=(m),
\]
division is exact and preserves the zero subsequence. Repetition proves T23.2.

Thus principal higher-growth geometry is the first genuine stratified zero-theorem subclass.

## 6. Corvaja–Zannier Proposition 3: exact T23 use

Corvaja and Zannier, Ann. Sci. Éc. Norm. Supér. 38 (2005), Proposition 3, prove the following small-value principle.

For an algebraic-coefficient analytic power series evaluated on an \(S\)-unit-type sequence satisfying their height/local-contraction balance, if
\[
|f(x_h)|_\nu
=
O(\|x_h\|_\nu^B)
\]
for **every fixed positive \(B\)**, then the sequence is eventually contained in finitely many torus cosets on which \(f\) vanishes. In particular \(f(x_h)\) is eventually zero.

This is stronger than exact-zero Theorem 3 for the T23 slow-face purpose.

T23 applies Proposition 3 only after projection to one balanced growth stratum. It does not reapply the theorem to the full unequal-growth point.

This distinction is load-bearing.

### 9.4 Truncation does not silently repair the problem

Suppose a slow analytic coefficient is truncated at degree \(D_n\).

The slow coordinate contraction is on scale \(g_{i-1}(n)\). To approximate the coefficient to precision on the next scale \(g_i(n)\), one needs schematically
\[
D_n\,g_{i-1}(n)\gtrsim g_i(n).
\]

But the height of the resulting algebraic truncation evaluated at the slow point is then also on the order
\[
D_n\,g_{i-1}(n)
=
\Omega(g_i(n)).
\]

Thus the moving target does not have height
\[
o(g_i(n)).
\]

The standard moving-target hypothesis is not recovered.

This is the exact moving-coefficient obstruction left by T23.
